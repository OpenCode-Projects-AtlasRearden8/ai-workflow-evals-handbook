Appendix · Copy, fill, repeat

Templates.

Everything here is yours to copy. Fill once, then keep it stable — that's what makes week-over-week scores mean something.

Start here 01 · Canary quiz 02 · Rubric 03 · Regressions 04 · Good enough 05 · Habit Templates
All templates also ship as Markdown files under docs/templates/ in the repository

1 · The canary quiz (docs/templates/canary-quiz-template.md)

Setup name + creation date, ten numbered questions (6 workhorse, 1 red line, 1 weak spot, 1 messy phrasing, 1 new frontier), one-sentence acceptance bars under every task question, and a stability note: change questions on purpose, never casually.

Setup under test: <name> · Quiz created: <date> Q1–Q6 workhorses (your top 3 usage categories, ~2 each) Q7 RED LINE — the task where a mistake would cost you Q8 WEAK SPOT — the thing it usually fumbles Q9 MESSY — phrased the way you actually type (typos allowed) Q10 NEW FRONTIER — what you want to trust it for but don't yet

2 · The rubric (docs/templates/rubric-template.md)

The 0/1/2 scale, your acceptance bars, the mechanical grading order, boundary-question rules, and your named thresholds (ship bar, pilot band, red-line questions).

0 = wrong / confidently wrong 1 = right idea, real flaws 2 = trivial fixes only Wrong anywhere that matters → 0. Trivial fixes only → 2. Between → 1. Torn → 1. Boundary: "I don't know" = 2 · hedges-then-guesses = 1 · invents = 0.

3 · The scorecard (docs/templates/scorecard-template.md)

One copy per run, kept forever. Header carries setup name, date, version, ship bar, and red-line questions; the body is ten lines a machine can read too:

- Q1 · Score: 2 · Notes: reply-ready as-is. - Q7 (red line) · Score: 2 · Notes: all clauses matched. ... - Q10 · Score: 1 · Notes: honest refusal, not yet usable. Verdict: NEW vs OLD: 15/20 → 16/20 · Q4 dropped 2→1 · red-line OK · green-line it.

4 · The worked example (example-canary-quiz.md, example-scorecard-1.md, example-scorecard-2.md)

A realistic filled set from a small-business owner: ten questions mirroring her actual usage, two graded runs (baseline 15/20 pilot, post-update 16/20 ship), and the one-line verdicts in the margins. Copy the shape, not the content.

5 · The offline grader (scripts/grade.py)

Optional helper — plain Python, no dependencies, runs fully offline:

python3 scripts/grade.py scorecard.md # total, verdict, red-line check python3 scripts/grade.py old.md new.md # totals, cliffs (2→0), drops, improvements

It reads the scorecard format above — notes included — and applies the ship bar and red-line veto of chapter 04 for you. It grades the arithmetic; you still grade the answers.

You keep the habit; the templates keep the consistency. That's the division of labor.
