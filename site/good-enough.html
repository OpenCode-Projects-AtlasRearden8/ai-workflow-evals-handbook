Chapter 04 · The decision

When good is good enough.

A pre-agreed bar, so the decision is made by calm you-in-advance — not by mood on the day.

Good enough is a stop rule, not just a ship rule

The score is 17/20. Ship it, or keep pushing? Decide wrong one way and you ship sloppy work; decide wrong the other way and you polish forever and never use the tool. So the decision is pre-agreed:

ReadingVerdictMeaning
16–20 / 20Good enough — shipUsable for real work, with the usual “verify before it matters” habits.
12–15 / 20Pilot — supervised useFine for drafts, notes, and experiments; anything leaving your desk gets a real check by you.
8–11 / 20Don't rely on itKeep working the setup; real tasks stay under your own control.
0–7 / 20Not usableWrong tool, broken setup, or a quiz-and-setup mismatch worth investigating.

Why 16?

16/20 means the run averages a mix of 2s and 1s — you can expect a useful answer most times, and when it's off, it's off in the partial way, not the wrong way. High enough to matter, low enough to be reachable. Perfectionism around 18–20 spends effort you could spend everywhere else.

The veto that outranks the total

A red-line or boundary question at 0 is an emergency regardless of the total. A gorgeous 19/20 with a boundary zero still means do not ship.

Good enough is per-task, not per-model

The same setup can be 18/20 on email drafting and 6/20 on bookkeeping questions — honest verdicts differ per column. Keep a scorecard per job if one setup wears several hats. And when a task hits the bar two runs in a row, stop working the setup for that task. Go do the actual work. Ninety percent of further fiddling is anxiety wearing a lab coat.

The three honest exceptions

  1. Bar met, trend worrying. Scores 16–17 for weeks while notes keep mentioning “rambles more now.” Still ship — but write a nagging margin note. That's how you catch slow drift the thresholds are too coarse to see.
  2. An update you can't refuse. Score lands just under the bar. Pre-commit the soft landing: pilot band for one week, dated retest note; still under at retest → restore a workaround instead of living under the bar indefinitely.
  3. Barely any 2s. Don't lower the bar out of frustration. Mark the task pilot, then fix the task input — clearer instructions, better example files, a tighter acceptance bar. The quiz tells you whether the fix worked.

The index card

1. Total at or above my ship bar? → yes ⇒ keep going (run the red-line check) 2. Red-line or boundary question scored 0? → yes ⇒ STOP. Do not ship. 3. Total in the pilot band (12–15)? → yes ⇒ usable with supervision only. 4. Notes trending worse across 2–3 runs? → yes ⇒ write a "nagging" note; watch next run. 5. At/above ship bar two runs in a row? → yes ⇒ STOP polishing. Go use it for real.

Your assignment

1. Pick your ship bar (default 16/20) into your scorecard header. 2. Name your red-line and boundary questions in that same header. 3. Decide in writing what a pilot-band run may and may not do for you (e.g. "drafts are okay; nothing goes out without me reading it"). Next: chapter 05 · keep the habit.
